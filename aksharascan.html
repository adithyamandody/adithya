Science Fair Project Proposal · 2026–27

AksharaScan Giving back the keystrokes that Malayalam takes away — a faster way to type with a single button, for people who have only one movement left.

Targets: IRIS National Fair (deadline 3 Oct 2026) → Regeneron ISEF · Kerala School Sasthrolsavam · Category: Systems Software
People with severe motor disabilities type using one button. A highlight scans through the letters; they press when it reaches the one they want. For English this has been optimised for decades. For Malayalam, nobody has done it — so a Malayalam speaker presses far more times to say the same thing. You are going to fix that, measure exactly how much you saved, and put it on a table where a judge can feel the difference with their own thumb.

1The problem, in thirty seconds

Imagine someone with cerebral palsy, ALS or a spinal injury. They cannot use a keyboard. They have one reliable movement — a tap on a large button, a blink, a twitch of one cheek muscle. That is their entire input to a computer.

So the computer does the work instead. It shows the alphabet and moves a highlight through it — one symbol at a time, or one row at a time. When the highlight reaches what they want, they press. This is called scanning, and it is how tens of thousands of people write every day.

It is slow. Every wasted step in the scan is a second of someone's life spent waiting to say a word.

The person you have already heard of

Stephen Hawking typed exactly like this. After losing his voice in 1985 he used a program called Equalizer: a cursor scanned rows and letters, and he squeezed a hand clicker to select. At his best, about 15 words per minute.

Then ALS took his hands. The clicker was replaced by an infrared sensor on his glasses watching a twitch in his cheek. Same brain, same software, worse switch — and his speed collapsed to one or two words per minute. In 2014 Intel rebuilt his system with word prediction trained on his own books and roughly doubled it again.

That collapse is this entire project in one biography. When each selection is slow and might misfire, the number of scan steps per word is everything.

2Why Malayalam is worse than English — and why that is a research question

English scanning is a solved optimisation problem. You arrange 26 letters so common ones need fewer steps; it is the same mathematics as file compression. Done, decades ago.

Malayalam is not a flat row of letters. It is an akshara script, and it has structure that English does not:

  • After a consonant like ക, the only things that can legally come next are a vowel sign (ാ ി ു…), a virama ്, an anusvara ം, or a small set of others. Most of the alphabet cannot follow it at all.
  • Two vowel signs never follow each other.
  • Conjuncts (koottaksharam) create hundreds of combined forms with no English equivalent.
  • Malayalam is agglutinative — endings pile onto words, so whole-word prediction works far worse than it does in English.

Every scanning system that ships today ignores all of this. Android Switch Access and iOS Switch Control simply scan whatever grid the keyboard happens to draw, in layout order, offering symbols that cannot possibly be typed next. No per-language optimisation exists for any Indian script.

The idea Build a scan tree that knows the rules of the script. After each symbol it offers only what can legally come next, ordered so the likely ones arrive first. Measure how many button presses that saves, across five Indian scripts, with English as a control.

3What the numbers look like

Take the sentence നീ സുഖം ആണോ — eleven selections including the two spaces. Here is a first-pass model of each method. (These are estimated frequencies, not corpus-measured; producing the real numbers is week one of the project.)

Method Presses Scan steps Time @ 1s
A — What ships today (row–column over a Malayalam grid)221151 min 55 s
B — Same grid, sorted by letter frequency228989 s
C — Entropy tree, ignores context366363 s
D — AksharaScan (context + legality constraint)204949 s

Two minutes today. Under a minute with your tree. The word സുഖം alone drops from roughly 45 seconds to 18.

Look at row C though — it is where the interesting engineering hides. A plain entropy tree is faster in steps but needs 36 presses instead of 22, which is worse for someone whose every press costs effort. AksharaScan gets both numbers down, because once the illegal options are removed the correct symbol is usually sitting in the very first group offered.

Add word prediction on top and that sentence should land near 30–35 seconds.

4What is genuinely new (the question a judge will ask first)

A good judge will immediately ask “hasn't someone done this?” Here is the honest answer, and knowing it cold is worth more than any extra feature.

Closest existing workWhat it didWhat it did not do
Higger et al., 2016
Optimal scan trees		Solved the optimal scanning tree mathematically for a flat alphabet.Assumes every symbol is legal at every step. Cannot express a legal set that changes as you go. This is your baseline, not your contribution.
Roark et al., 2013
Huffman scanning		Rebuilds the tree after every character using a language model — a soft version of context.No hard legality rule; never tested on a non-alphabetic script. This is the baseline you must beat.
Android Switch Access / iOS Switch ControlShip worldwide, free, on every phone.Scan whatever grid is drawn, in layout order. No per-language optimisation, no akshara awareness, no language model.
Avaz, Jellow (Indian AAC apps)Ship Malayalam; Jellow supports external switches.Neither publishes an optimised per-language scan tree. Good news — there is a real ecosystem that could adopt yours.
Say this, not something stronger

“Context-conditional scan trees already exist. What has never been done is treating akshara legality as a hard constraint, and nobody has measured whether that hard constraint buys anything over a soft language model — or whether the benefit grows as the script gets more complex. I built it, I built the control that isolates it, and I measured it on five Brahmic scripts with English as a negative control.”

Overclaiming is how projects die in the first thirty seconds. This sentence is defensible and still exciting.

5What you actually build

1. The simulator (the science)

Python on your own laptop. Types thousands of real sentences through every method and counts presses. Free public corpora. No hardware. This is the main result.

2. The constraint engine (the idea)

Learns which units can legally follow which, from text. Feeds the tree builder so only legal options are ever offered.

3. Two one-button boxes (the demo)

Box A scans the old way. Box B runs AksharaScan. One big button each, a large press counter on top.

4. The evidence video

Screen recording of real Android Switch Access struggling with a koottaksharam. Proof your baseline is real, not invented.

6The experiments

You compare eight scanning methods, on six languages, over real text. The comparison itself is the science; the boxes are how you show it.

MethodWhat it is
C0–C2Linear scan, the real shipped grid, and a frequency-sorted grid. The baselines.
C3Static entropy-optimised tree, no context (Higger-style).
C3bContext-conditional tree, no legality rule (Roark re-implemented). The baseline you must beat.
C4AksharaScan — context + hard legality constraint. Your contribution.
C5 / C6C4 plus word prediction, versus C4 plus morpheme prediction. Tests whether Malayalam's agglutination breaks whole-word prediction.

Languages: Malayalam, Kannada, Tamil, Hindi, Bengali — plus English as a negative control. English has almost no legality structure, so if your method helps English as much as it helps Malayalam, the constraint is not doing what you claim. That control is what makes the result believable.

The four claims

  1. C4 beats C3b on all five Brahmic scripts, by more than 3%. The headline.
  2. The “akshara tax”: naive Malayalam versus naive English on identical sentences — predicted 1.3–1.8×. A defensible 1.5× beats an indefensible 3×.
  3. A scaling law: does the benefit grow with script complexity across six languages?
  4. Morphemes beat words for Malayalam prediction, by 5% or more.
The part that makes this safe to attempt

The project has four independent layers, and each one is a complete result on its own. Even if the main claim underperforms, you still have: the measured akshara tax, the audit of what Android actually does with Malayalam, and the morphology result. And a negative result is still a real finding — “hard constraints add little over a soft model in Brahmic scripts” is publishable, and you would rather be the person who reported it.

Nothing on the critical path needs a delivery, a partner, a lab, a permission or good weather. It is your laptop and free text.

8Where this goes

IRIS National Fair is India's only route to Regeneron ISEF, and Class 12 is your last eligible year. It has a Systems Software category, which is exactly where an algorithm with a measured claim belongs. Winners represent India at ISEF in Los Angeles in May 2027.

Sasthrolsavam gets the same work as a Working Model: two boxes, one button each, the judge types the same sentence twice and watches the counters diverge. Cheap, socially relevant, visibly working — the three things those judges actually reward.

And independently of any fair: put the code and the scan trees on Zenodo with a DOI, then open an issue on Jellow's tracker offering them. A real Malayalam AAC app exists that could adopt this. That is the outcome most likely to make the work genuinely known — and it does not depend on winning anything.

One rule that is not negotiable

This project has zero human participants, by design. No survey, no volunteer typing trial, no visits to a special school to collect data. The moment you record anyone else's press count or reaction — especially a disabled person or a minor — it becomes human-subjects research needing written approval before collection, which is never granted retroactively. It would surface in November and disqualify everything.

You may absolutely talk to a special educator and thank them in the acknowledgements. You may let anyone try the boxes at the fair. You simply never write down what they did.
